1. Dataset Description

This lab explores the 2025 nominal Gross Domestic Product (GDP) for the world's 50 largest economies based on the IMF World Economic Outlook (October 2025) database. The dataset includes country ISO-3 identifiers, standardized country names, projected nominal GDP in billions of U.S. dollars, and economic ranks.

Countries outside the top 50 are represented in neutral light gray to signify "No Data" rather than zero economic output, preventing misleading visual interpretations.

4. Design and Comparison

In the choropleth, geographically extensive nations such as Russia, Canada, Australia, and Brazil naturally dominate spatial attention through sheer physical footprint, while the United States and China dominate the color luminance channel with the deepest saturation. In the cartogram, the United States and China expand into commanding visual anchors. However, the most striking change is the inflation of compact yet affluent economies such as Singapore, Switzerland, Ireland, the Netherlands, and Hong Kong SAR ,which grow to sizes comparable with Germany or India. Conversely, land-rich nations with relatively smaller economies like Russia, Canada, Kazakhstan, Algeria, and Nigeria shrink sharply, revealing that their geographic area vastly exceeds their economic weight.

The choropleth preserves coastlines, borders, adjacency, and regional clustering, but it under-represents compact high-GDP economies. The cartogram distorts shape, orientation, and territory, but encodes GDP directly as perceivable area.

Consequently, the choropleth best supports spatial exploration tasks, such as assessing geopolitical contiguity or regional economic patterns across Western Europe and Southeast Asia. The cartogram best supports quantitative magnitude comparisons, such as estimating the economic disparity between rank-3 and rank-15 economies without landmass bias. Switching from color encoding to area encoding fundamentally transforms perception that global economic output is not spread across spread across continents, but is intensely concentrated within a handful of high-output economies.
