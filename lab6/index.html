Design Description

In both treemaps I encode GDP amount with rectangle area and GDP status with color. Area is the standard visual channel for part-to-whole comparison — the eye judges relative size accurately — and color is ideal for the three discrete status categories, which also gives a clear legend. Each treemap draws the full World → Continent → Area → Country hierarchy so the reader can compare continents and areas at a glance while still seeing individual countries inside them.

The two treemaps differ only in their spatial subdivision. Treemap A uses squarify, which optimizes aspect ratios and produces near-square rectangles. This makes country-level size comparison easier because the eye can judge area more precisely. Treemap B uses the binary algorithm, which recursively splits the space into two groups. It produces longer, more irregular rectangles but often respects the parent–child grouping more clearly, making the continent and area structure easier to trace. Comparing the two shows how the same data and aggregation can be re-segmented without changing the underlying information.
