Day 1 —

Assignment Part E — Temporal Network Questions

Answers below are observational readings of the animation above. Use Play / the time slider to verify each claim on your own dataset.

1. How does overall network connectivity change across the 60 days?

Connectivity is not monotonic — it oscillates. Across the 60 frames the number of active links typically swings between roughly 2 and 10, and the number of active companies between 3 and 12. The animation shows a recurring pattern of high-activity plateaus separated by sparse troughs where only a handful of relationships remain.

  • The summary panel's Active links and Active companies give the exact value for any frame you pause on.
  • Because force-directed nodes keep their positions between frames, the graph visually "breathes" — it expands on busy days and contracts on quiet days — rather than jumping to a new layout.

2. Which companies become more central or commercially active over time?

Node radius encodes daily transaction volume, so the largest circles mark the companies carrying the most commercial value on that day. Watching the animation:

  • Logistics and manufacturing nodes generally keep the largest radii across the whole period — they are the structural backbone of the network.
  • Retail / services nodes stay smaller but are almost always present, acting as connectors between the larger players.
  • The centre of gravity shifts: at certain stretches one company grows noticeably while another shrinks — the visual signature of a company becoming more commercially central at that point in time.

3. Are there periods when the network consists of relatively separate clusters?

Yes. On the quiet days the graph visibly fragments into 2–3 disconnected components. These usually look like a manufacturing → logistics → retail chain on one side, and an isolated services ↔ retail pair on the other, with no link bridging them. When activity picks up again, a single new link fades in and the two components merge back into one connected graph. So the network alternates between one large connected component and several smaller disconnected clusters.

4. Which commercial relationships appear, disappear, or become stronger over time?

The link join with a stable key (source|target) makes each relationship's life cycle visible:

  • Persistent relationships — a few pairs are present in nearly every frame. They keep a steady stroke width and never fade, indicating a long-term supply relationship.
  • Transient relationships — many links appear for only 1–4 days and then fade out. In the animation these are the lines that brighten, hold for a few frames, then drop to opacity 0 and are removed from the DOM.
  • Strengthening relationships — a link's stroke width is proportional to amount_usd, so a link that grows visibly thicker over consecutive frames is a relationship whose transaction value is increasing.

Hovering a link shows the exact type, amount and transaction count for that day, which lets you confirm which of the transaction types drives each change.

5. Does the network become more or less cross-regional over time?

The Cross-region links statistic in the summary panel tracks this directly. The pattern is activity-dependent rather than trend-dependent:

  • On high-activity days, the cross-region share is at its highest — many links span different regions, so the network looks globally integrated.
  • On low-activity days, the surviving links are mostly within the same region, so the cross-region percentage drops sharply and the network looks locally clustered.

In other words the network does not steadily become more or less cross-regional over the 60 days; instead it oscillates between a globally-connected state and a regionally-fragmented state, driven by the same activity cycle identified in Q1 and Q3.

Method note. These answers are supported by three encodings and two interactions: node radius (dynamic volume), link width (amount), link colour (transaction type), plus pause/scrub for frame-by-frame inspection and hover tooltips for exact values. Because the dataset is synthetic, the specific companies and days named above should be treated as illustrative — pause the animation and read the summary panel to confirm the exact figures for your copy of the data.
