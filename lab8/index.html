1. Bulletin and Corpus

The corpus is the Bulletin of Duke Kunshan University Undergraduate Instruction, 2021–22 edition. The file is V2021-22_DKU_UG_Bulletin.pdf (PDF dated 23 August 2021, 400 pages). The same edition is published at the DKU admissions copy. Date accessed: 27 September 2026.

One paragraph, or one short policy block, is one passage. A word, a page, or a whole chapter is not a unit. Each passage keeps chapter, section, subsection, page, and text. Chapter is the Part heading. Section is the next heading in the bulletin outline. Subsection is the smaller heading, including a course title when the passage is a course description.

5. Semantic Findings

The counts below are the 1,208 passages drawn on this page. A page number is not printed on the dot. Click the point and the detail panel shows Page. The two similarity scores are the cosine similarity of neighbor pairs the panel lists. The chart itself does not print that score.

What are the major semantic topics?

The legend has eight colors. The larger groups are course fields: Natural Science and Computation (211 passages), Politics and Global Affairs (203), Environment, Health, and Policy (164), Media and Arts (153), and China, History, and Culture (129). Language and Communication is smaller (83). Most of those points are Mandarin, writing, and skills courses. Two colors are rules rather than majors: Credits, Load, and Grading (166) and University Policy and Transfer Credit (99). Most points come from Part 10, Majors and Courses, so the field colors take up more of the map. The gold and dark-blue points are mostly in the earlier policy parts.

Which topics appear across multiple formal sections?

In the matrix, Credits, Load, and Grading appears in 41 sections, and 22 of those sections have at least two passages in that color. University Policy and Transfer Credit appears in 57 sections, and 15 of those have at least two passages. Those two columns are light marks down many rows. The field columns are different. Politics and Global Affairs is in only four sections, and almost all of its passages are in Course Descriptions or Majors. Science, China, Environment, and Media follow the same pattern: dark cells in the first two rows, and empty cells in most policy rows.

Which formal sections contain the most semantically diverse content?

Course Descriptions has all eight colors, across 630 passages. Majors has six colors, across 300 passages. Those two sections are catalogs, so the mix is what you would expect from a list of different subjects. The shorter sections mix less. Key Components has five topics in 14 passages. Degree Requirements has four topics in 23 passages. Registration is mostly one color: four of its five passages are Credits, Load, and Grading.

Which passages from different sections are semantically similar?

Pages 57 and 59 are two sections, one for the classes of 2022–2024 and one for the class of 2025 and beyond. Both say that a student whose record shows marginal scholarship “will be subject either to academic warning or academic probation.” Both points are Credits, Load, and Grading. Click either point and the other passage is in the five nearest neighbors. Their cosine similarity is 0.995.

Another pair comes from different parts. On page 75, in Office of Global Education and Study Away, one passage says students may transfer up to 40 credits taken off campus. On page 25, under Undergraduate Signature Work, the credit rules include “no more than 40 credits combining any allowable transfer credits,” along with the 136 credits required for the degree. Both points are University Policy and Transfer Credit, and their similarity is 0.86. One passage is filed with study away. The other is filed with degree requirements.

Which passages appear unusual relative to their formal section?

Partners has seven passages. Five of them describe Duke, Wuhan University, and Kunshan, and those are University Policy and Transfer Credit. Page 12 is the exception: “Its campus is regarded as one of the most beautiful in the country, boasting palatial buildings that blend Eastern and Western architectural styles.” That point is Media and Arts.

Estimated Expenses has 18 passages. Fifteen are Credits, Load, and Grading, mostly tuition and housing amounts. Page 32 is about a flight to Shanghai, visa costs, and “registration costs associated with establishing legal residence in China.” That point is China, History, and Culture.

On page 36, Academic Integrity has two passages under one heading. The passage about signing the Community Standard is University Policy and Transfer Credit. The next passage, about faculty consulting the Dean on academic misconduct, is Credits, Load, and Grading.

Do credit, graduation, registration, and academic integrity occupy one region?

No. Searching credit leaves 126 passages bright, in six colors. 78 are Credits, Load, and Grading, and 37 are University Policy and Transfer Credit. A few fall in Language, China, Science, or Environment. Degree credit and transfer credit are not the same part of the map. Searching graduation leaves 28 passages, and only those two policy colors: 16 and 12.

Searching registration leaves 8 passages. Five are Credits, Load, and Grading, including the registration instructions on pages 41 and 42. Two are University Policy and Transfer Credit: Compliance with Academic Regulations, and International Student Services. The last one is the page 32 travel paragraph. There, registration means the cost of legal residence in China, so the point is China, History, and Culture.

The exact words academic integrity appear twice. On page 36, in the Community Standard passage (University Policy and Transfer Credit), faculty are told to deal fairly with a possible violation of academic integrity. On page 66, in Procedure for Resolution of Students’ Academic Concerns (Credits, Load, and Grading), integrity cases are sent to the Undergraduate Academic Integrity Policy. The word integrity also appears in Who We Are, the Community Standard, and Student Conduct. Those passages are mostly University Policy and Transfer Credit. The two exact matches are not the same color, and neither one sits with the course fields.

6. Design Description

Each cleaned passage was embedded with all-MiniLM-L6-v2, a sentence-transformer model, without stop-word removal or lemmatization. The model keeps the whole sentence, so function words stay in. Every passage becomes a normalized 384-dimensional vector, and cosine similarity is the measure of closeness. K-means with eight clusters and random seed 401 was fit to those original vectors, not to the two-dimensional coordinates. UMAP then laid the same vectors out for the map, using cosine distance, 15 neighbors, a minimum distance of 0.15, and seed 401. The axes are only a layout. Nearness is what can be read, and it can be checked by opening a point and reading its five nearest neighbors.

Labels were assigned after inspection rather than copied from the bulletin’s headings. For each cluster I read passages near the center and the terms with the highest average TF-IDF. Those cues produced names such as Credits, Load, and Grading and China, History, and Culture. TF-IDF helps name a cluster. It does not place the points.

On the map, position is the UMAP projection, color is the topic, and point area is passage length in words. The matrix uses formal sections as rows and topics as columns. A darker cell means more passages, and the tooltip adds the count and the section’s share. Clicking a cell highlights those passages on the map. Clicking a point highlights its matrix cell and lists neighbors that may sit in another section. That link is how the outline and the semantic neighborhoods answer the same question: where a passage was filed, and which other passages mean something similar.
